# Stakeholder Role Hierarchy Classification Framework

## Purpose

This framework defines explicit role hierarchy classification for stakeholder analysis. It establishes clear hierarchical relationships between category layers and concrete roles, ensuring systematic stakeholder modeling across all cases.

---

## Hierarchy Structure Overview

### Three-Level Classification System

```
Level 1: Category Layer (抽象类别层)
    ↓
Level 2: Subcategory Layer (子类别层)
    ↓
Level 3: Concrete Role Layer (具体角色层)
```

**Rationale**: Three-level structure provides:
- **Category Layer**: Cross-industry abstraction for comparison
- **Subcategory Layer**: Industry-specific or functional grouping
- **Concrete Role Layer**: Specific roles from source documents

---

## Level 1: Category Layer (抽象类别层)

### 1. Decision Maker (决策者)

**Definition**: Stakeholders with final decision authority or budget approval power.

**Key Characteristics**:
- Final decision authority
- Budget approval power
- Strategic decision-making
- High influence level
- ROI and business value focus

**Hierarchy Position**: Top-level decision authority

---

### 2. IT Lead (IT领导者)

**Definition**: Stakeholders responsible for technical architecture, IT operations, and technology strategy.

**Key Characteristics**:
- Technical decision authority
- Architecture responsibility
- Operations management
- High technical influence
- Feasibility and implementation focus

**Hierarchy Position**: Technical leadership below decision makers

---

### 3. Operator (运营者)

**Definition**: Stakeholders responsible for daily operations, execution, and system maintenance.

**Key Characteristics**:
- Daily operational responsibility
- System execution and maintenance
- Incident handling and support
- Medium-high influence (operational)
- Efficiency and execution focus

**Hierarchy Position**: Operational execution layer

---

### 4. User (最终用户)

**Definition**: Stakeholders who are direct users of the system or service.

**Key Characteristics**:
- Direct system usage
- Experience-based perspective
- User satisfaction focus
- Medium influence (through feedback)
- Usability and experience focus

**Hierarchy Position**: End-user layer

---

### 5. Regulator (监管者)

**Definition**: Stakeholders responsible for policy, compliance, and regulatory oversight.

**Key Characteristics**:
- Regulatory authority
- Compliance enforcement
- Standard setting
- High constraint (not influence)
- Risk and compliance focus

**Hierarchy Position**: External constraint layer

---

### 6. Partner (合作伙伴)

**Definition**: External stakeholders providing support, services, or partnership.

**Key Characteristics**:
- External to organization
- Service provision
- Advisory or support role
- Medium influence
- Collaboration and service focus

**Hierarchy Position**: External support layer

## Role Classification Rules

### Rule 1: Primary Category Assignment

**Rule**: Each role belongs to exactly one primary category.

**Classification Priority**:
1. Decision authority → Decision Maker
2. Technical leadership → IT Lead
3. Operations execution → Operator
4. System usage → User
5. Regulatory/Compliance → Regulator
6. External provider → Partner

### Rule 2: Role-Level Determination

**Rule**: Determine hierarchy level based on scope and authority.

| Authority Level | Position in Hierarchy | Examples |
|----------------|---------------------|-----------|
| Enterprise-wide | Executive Leadership | CEO, CIO, CTO |
| Business Unit | Strategic Management | VP, Director |
| Functional | Subcategory Lead | Infrastructure Manager |
| Operational | Concrete Role | Network Admin, IT Support |

### Rule 3: Cross-Hierarchy Roles

**Rule**: Some roles may have cross-hierarchy responsibilities.

**Handling**:
- Assign primary category based on main function
- Note secondary responsibilities
- Document dual-role cases
- Use confidence level for assignment

### Rule 4: Context-Specific Roles

**Rule**: Role meaning varies by industry and organization.

**Handling**:
- Consider industry context
- Consider organization size
- Consider organizational structure
- Document contextual factors
- Use confidence level for classification

## Best Practices

1. **Always Use Three-Level Structure**: Category → Subcategory → Concrete Role
2. **Document Industry Context**: Roles vary significantly by industry
3. **Assign Confidence**: Not all classifications are equally certain
4. **Provide Basis**: Always explain why a role was classified as such
5. **Handle Ambiguity**: Note when roles have cross-hierarchy responsibilities
6. **Use Bilingual Quotes**: Translate non-Chinese source quotes
7. **Validate Against Hierarchy**: Ensure role fits the established hierarchy
8. **Track Role Variants**: Different titles may refer to same role type
